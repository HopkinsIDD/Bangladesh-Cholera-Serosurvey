# *Vibrio cholerae* O1 transmission in Bangladesh: insights from a nationally representative serosurvey

Code and data to reproduce the analyses in:

> Azman AS, Lauer SA, Bhuiyan MTR, et al. *Vibrio cholerae* O1 transmission in Bangladesh: insights from a nationally representative serosurvey. **Lancet Microbe** 2020; 1: e336–43. [doi:10.1016/S2666-5247(20)30141-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2666-5247(20)30141-5)

The pipeline starts from de-identified, cleaned serosurvey data and produces the
national and 5 km × 5 km grid-cell seroincidence estimates, the risk-factor
models, the supplement and Figures 1–2.

> **Earlier version.** Until September 2026 this repository held the code for the
> March 2020 medRxiv preprint ([doi:10.1101/2020.03.13.20035352](https://doi.org/10.1101/2020.03.13.20035352)),
> which reported a national seroincidence of 19.1% rather than the published 17.3%.
> That version is kept under the git tag `medrxiv-v1`.

## Quick start

```bash
git clone https://github.com/HopkinsIDD/Bangladesh-Cholera-Serosurvey.git
cd Bangladesh-Cholera-Serosurvey
bash inst/run-analysis.sh
```

`inst/run-analysis.sh` runs every step below in order. It takes about
3 hours on a 15-core laptop. You can also run the steps one at a time
(always from the repository root).

## Requirements

- **R ≥ 4.3** (last tested with R 4.5.3 on macOS, September 2026).
- **R packages:** installed by `source/install-packages-dl-data.R`, including
  [INLA](https://www.r-inla.org/download-install) and two GitHub-only packages
  (`INLAutils`, `captioner`).
- **A working [rstan](https://mc-stan.org/rstan/) toolchain** (a C++ compiler; on macOS, the Xcode command line tools).
  With some CRAN binary combinations, compiled models fail to load with
  `symbol not found ... tbb::task_scheduler_init`. If that happens, install
  StanHeaders 2.32.x and RcppParallel 5.1.x, which rstan 2.32 was built against:
  `remotes::install_version("StanHeaders", "2.32.10"); remotes::install_version("RcppParallel", "5.1.9")`,
  then reinstall rstan from source.
- **pandoc and LaTeX** to render the reports to PDF. Both come with RStudio; for
  LaTeX alone, `tinytex::install_tinytex()` is enough.
- **Memory:** 8 GB is enough (the Stan step peaks at about 4 GB).

## Pipeline

| Step | Script | Output (in `generated_data/`) | Time* |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `source/install-packages-dl-data.R` | installs packages; downloads cohort data to `data/bgd-cohort.rds` | minutes |
| 2 | `source/generate-analyses-dataset.R [RERUN=T] [USE_PUBLISHED_RF=T]` | `dat_with_raster_covs.rds`, `analysis_age_props.rds`, `cohort-w-rf-preds-*.csv`, `forest_no_ogrp_*.rds` | ~1 h |
| 3 | `source/bgd-estimates-stan.R <days> <rf\|vib> youden <chains> <seed>` for 365/200/100 days × rf/vib | `results-{rf,vib}-{365,200,100}*.rds` | 3–5 min each |
| 4 | `source/sampling_uncert_gridmap.R <1-1000> <main\|season>` | `boot-maps/`, `boot-maps-season/` (1,000 INLA maps each) | ~30 min per map type (6 in parallel) |
| 5 | `source/loocv-analysis.R` | `loocv-results.rds` | ~5 min |
| 6 | `source/risk_factors_and_model_comp.Rmd` | `covariate_ests.csv`, `tables/table1.docx`, figures | ~1 min |
| 7 | `source/supplement.Rmd` | supplement PDF; `grid-cell-data*.rds`, `INLA-seroincidence-est*.rds`, `bgd2-seroincidence*.rds` | ~5 min |
| 8 | `source/manuscript-estimates.Rmd` | numbers quoted in the paper; `figures/fig1_raw.*`, `figures/fig2_raw.*` | minutes |

\*Wall-clock times on an Apple M-series laptop with 15 cores.

`generated_data/` in this repository holds only the final summaries needed to check the paper's numbers without rerunning anything:
`INLA-seroincidence-est*.rds` (national estimates for each posterior map),
`grid-cell-data*.rds` (grid-cell estimates), `bgd2-seroincidence*.rds` (district estimates),
`loocv-results.rds` and `covariate_ests.csv`.
Everything else (posterior draws, maps, fitted forests) is regenerated by the pipeline and git-ignored.
To re-render the supplement or `manuscript-estimates.Rmd`, run steps 2–3 first.

What each step does:

1. **Random forest classification** (step 2). A random forest is trained on a Bangladeshi cohort of confirmed
   cholera cases and their household contacts ([Azman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aau6242)).
   Predictors are age, sex, Inaba and Ogawa vibriocidal titres, anti-LPS IgG and IgA, and anti-CTB IgG and IgA.
   The forest classifies each serosurvey participant as infected or not in the previous 100, 200 or 365 days.
   Leave-one-individual-out cross-validation on the cohort gives the predictions used to estimate sensitivity and specificity.
2. **Bayesian hierarchical model** (step 3; `source/sens-spec.stan`, `source/random-effects.stan`).
   This step first estimates time-varying sensitivity and specificity with individual random effects.
   It then fits individual-level seropositivity with community and household random effects,
   adjusting for sensitivity and specificity, and post-stratifies each community to its age and sex structure (WorldPop 2015).
   For the 365-day random forest, the model is refit with community-specific sensitivity weighted by local cholera seasonality
   (`source/random-effects-seasonality.stan`).
3. **Spatial model** (steps 4–5). For each of 1,000 posterior draws, an INLA model with a Matérn spatial field is fit
   to the post-stratified community estimates and predicted to every grid cell.
   Population-weighted averages give the national estimate. Leave-one-community-out cross-validation compares this model with covariate and naive models.
4. **Reports** (steps 6–8). Risk-factor models, the supplement, and the main-text numbers and figures.

## Data

All files in `data/` are de-identified or derived from public sources.
Participant, household and community ids (`sample_id` S0001–S2930, `hhid` H001–H707,
`community_id` 101–170, and `CommunityID` in `sampled_communities.csv`) are new ids
that keep the original sort order; they are not the study ids.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `serosurvey-dat.rds` | One row per participant (2,930 people, 707 households, 70 communities): ids, collection date, antibody measurements (see below), age, sex, household survey variables used as risk factors, household GPS coordinates, 5 km grid cell, and `vib_320` (max vibriocidal titre ≥ 320, as used in the paper) |
| `community-data.rds` | The 70 sampled communities: census codes, centroid, census population (`pop_both`) and grid cell |
| `daily-infection-probs.rds` | For each participant, the probability of infection on each of the 100/200/365 days before sampling given infection in that window. Estimated from sentinel cholera surveillance and acute watery diarrhoea reports (`est_cholera_sentinel()` and `estimate_daily_probs()` in `source/raw-data/raw-data-utils.R`) |
| `published-rf-predictions.rds`, `published-cohort-w-rf-preds-365-youden.csv` | Random forest predictions from the published analysis (see *Reproducibility notes*) |
| `bgd-cohort.rds` | Cohort data for training the random forest, from [scottyaz/cross-sectional-cholera-sero](https://github.com/scottyaz/cross-sectional-cholera-sero) |
| `grid_with_covs.rds` | 5 km × 5 km grid over Bangladesh (cells with population > 0): WorldPop 2015 population, proportion female and age structure, distance to major water body, poverty index, friction surface, travel time to cities, altitude |
| `all_age_props.rds` | WorldPop 2015 population by sex and age group (0, 1–4, then 5-year groups to 80+) for each grid cell |
| `bgd2-pop-2015.rds` | WorldPop 2015 population of each district (`BGD_ppp_2015_adj_v2.tif` summed over GADM level-2 polygons) |
| `census_2011_3.dta`, `sampled_communities.csv`, `division-zila-codes.csv` | 2011 census community populations and codes used for the sampling frame |
| `census_data_sex_age.csv` | National age–sex distribution (population pyramid figure) |
| `BGD_adm{0,1,2,3}.rds` | GADM administrative boundaries |

### Antibody measurements

The serosurvey measured eight *V. cholerae*-specific assays:

- Vibriocidal titres against Inaba and Ogawa (`vibinab`, `vibogaw`).
- ELISA IgA and IgG against Inaba LPS and Ogawa LPS (`lps_iga_inaba`, `lps_iga_ogawa`, `lps_igg_inaba`, `lps_igg_ogawa`).
  `lps_iga` and `lps_igg` are the per-person maximum of Inaba and Ogawa.
- ELISA IgA and IgG against cholera toxin B subunit (`ctb_iga`, `ctb_igg`).

**No IgM was measured in the serosurvey.** The published Methods say "isotype-specific (IgM and IgG)"; that should read IgA and IgG.
The cohort data do include anti-LPS and anti-CTB IgM (`plpsm_s`, `pmctxb_s`), but the random forest uses only predictors measured in both datasets.

### Raw data

The raw lab files, full questionnaire and sentinel surveillance line lists are not public; contact azman@jhu.edu.
`source/raw-data/` documents how the public inputs were built from them.
Those scripts cannot be run from this repository.

## Reproducibility notes

Results from a full run of `inst/run-analysis.sh` (September 2026), compared with the published paper:

| Quantity | Published | This repository |
|---|---|---|
| National annual seroincidence (INLA) | 17.3% (10.5–24.1) | 17.5% (10.5–24.3) |
| Infections in the year before the survey | 28.1 M (17.1–39.2) | 28.5 M (17.0–39.6) |
| Community seroincidence range (post-stratified) | 4.3%–62.9% | 4.3%–64.0% |
| Communities < 5% / > 50% | 2 / 2 | 3 / 2‡ |
| Households with no seropositive member | 339 (48%) | 339 (47.9%) |
| Risk ratio if another household member seropositive | 1.70 (1.46–1.98) | 1.70 (1.46–1.98) |
| ICC household / community | 39.9% / 34.2% | 40.0% / 35.0% |
| Children < 10 y vs ≥ 20 y, adjusted OR | 0.49 (0.34–0.70) | 0.48 (0.34–0.70) |
| Grid-cell relative risk range | 0.34–3.02 | 0.35–3.02 |
| Dhaka district: seroincidence | 14.4% (4.5–28.4) | 14.3% (3.8–30.1) |
| Dhaka district: infections (share of national) | 2.0 M (0.6–3.9); 7.0% | 2.0 M (0.5–4.1); 6.9% |
| Post-stratified survey estimate, 365 d random forest (Stan)† | 19.9% (15.1–25.0) | 20.0% (15.0–25.3) |
| Same, with seasonality† | 20.1% (15.5–24.6) | 20.2% (15.7–24.6) |

†From the original analysis outputs, not quoted in the main text. The vibriocidal and 100/200-day models, and the risk-factor odds ratios (largest difference 0.02 on the log scale), agree similarly.
‡The third community's median is 4.98%, just under the 5% cutoff.

These are the outputs committed in `generated_data/`. Repeated runs vary through Monte Carlo error: an earlier full run gave 17.3% (11.0–24.3) and 28.1 M (18.0–39.6).

Small differences are expected:

- **MCMC and INLA.** Stan and INLA versions, and Monte Carlo error, differ from 2020.
- **Random forest.** Refitting the forests with current packages reproduces 99.3–100% of individual predictions, not all of them.
  The 365-day raw positivity is 20.4% refit vs 19.9% published.
  By default step 2 still refits, but then swaps in the published predictions (`data/published-rf-predictions.rds`) so later steps match the paper.
  Run `Rscript source/generate-analyses-dataset.R TRUE FALSE` to use the refit predictions instead.
- **`vib_320`.** In the published analysis this flag came from the originally reported vibriocidal titres, not the cleaned titres in `vibinab`/`vibogaw`.
  It differs for 19 people, so it is provided as a column rather than recalculated.
- **Seasonality model.** `source/random-effects-seasonality.stan` was never committed to the original analysis repository.
  It was reconstructed from how `bgd-estimates-stan.R` calls it, and it reproduces the published seasonality estimates (see table).
  The seasonality refit now runs only for the 365-day random forest, the only one reported.
  For 200 days, one chain stalled indefinitely on its first warm-up iteration.
- **Covariate map.** A supplementary map with covariates in the spatial model (`boot-maps-alt`) was not reported in the paper and is not included.
